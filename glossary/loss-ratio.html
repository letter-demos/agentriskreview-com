Glossary

Loss Ratio

Loss ratio is the share of premium a carrier pays out in claims, and one of the measures carriers use to judge the quality of an agent's book.

Loss ratio is the amount an insurer pays in claims, often including claim adjustment expenses, divided by the premium it earns, expressed as a percentage. A book of business that earns $100,000 in premium and incurs $60,000 in losses has a 60% loss ratio.

Carriers track loss ratios by agent and agency as a measure of how well a producer selects and places risks. A consistently high loss ratio can lead a carrier to restrict an agent's binding authority or end an appointment, while a stable, favourable trend supports requests to expand into new lines.

Looking at the trend over several years tells a carrier more than a single year's figure, since one large claim can distort a short period.
