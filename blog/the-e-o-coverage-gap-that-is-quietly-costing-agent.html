The E&O Coverage Gap That Is Quietly Costing Agents Carrier Appointments

Errors and omissions coverage is non-negotiable for any professional insurance agent, and most agents know that. What is less understood is how carriers evaluate E&O history during the appointment process, and how even a short coverage gap years in the past can complicate a submission that otherwise looks strong.

When a carrier reviews an agent's compliance documentation, they typically want to see continuous E&O coverage going back several years. The specific lookback period varies by carrier and line of business, but three to five years is common. What they are looking for is not just that you have coverage today, but that you have maintained it consistently. A gap of even thirty to sixty days can trigger additional questions and, in some cases, can result in a declined appointment or a restricted binding authority agreement.

The reasons carriers care about this go beyond the obvious. An agent who allowed E&O coverage to lapse, even briefly, is signaling something about how they manage their own business. Carriers make inferences from that. If an agent did not prioritize protecting themselves from liability, what does that suggest about how carefully they handle client coverage and documentation? It is an uncomfortable question, but it is the question underwriters are quietly asking.

The solution is straightforward in principle but requires some legwork in practice. Agents should maintain organized records of every E&O policy they have held, including declarations pages, effective and expiration dates, and renewal confirmations. If there was a gap, it is better to disclose it proactively with context than to have a carrier discover it and wonder what else is missing. A third-party review that documents your coverage history and addresses any gaps directly will almost always serve you better than leaving carriers to draw their own conclusions.

If you are not sure whether your E&O history is fully documented, now is the time to find out. Carriers are not becoming more lenient on this point, and the cost of getting your documentation in order is a fraction of the cost of a declined appointment or a delayed expansion into a new market.
